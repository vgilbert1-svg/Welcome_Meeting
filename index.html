Course overview

Cambridge Lower Secondary Computing, Stage 9

Three periods per week, using the Hodder Computing 9 textbook. Every lesson is linked to a Cambridge learning objective in one of five strands.

9P · 12 OBJECTIVES

Programming

Python programs with data types, loops, arrays and strings, tested with test plans and trace tables.

9CT · 9 OBJECTIVES

Computational thinking

Flowcharts, pseudocode, iteration and binary searches.

9CS · 11 OBJECTIVES

Computer systems

Operating systems, translators, logic circuits, machine learning and Industry 4.0.

9MD · 8 OBJECTIVES

Managing data

Spreadsheet models, relational databases and Big Data.

9DC · 5 OBJECTIVES

Networks and digital communication

Topologies, protocols, scalability, parity checks and security.

Assessment and grading

How your child is assessed

EVERY UNIT

End of Unit project and/or test

Six continual assessments in the year (As#1 to As#6), covering every learning objective in the unit, for each student.

MIDTERM GRADE

An average, not a separate test

There is no midterm exam in Computing. The midterm grade is the average of the unit assessments completed that semester.

END OF SEMESTER

Standardised exams

The same exam across the system. Semester 1 covers Units 1–3. Semester 2 (End of Year) covers Units 1–6.

  • All end of semester Computing exams are pen and paper, so students practise both on their device and on paper.
  • Grades are published on School Online, where parents can see them.
  • Digital Citizenship has no summative assessment.

Fair assessment

Quiz rules and profile locking

  • Copying or pasting during a quiz, including text from AI tools, locks the student’s profile.
  • Opening developer tools during a quiz also locks the profile.
  • Leaving the quiz tab gives one warning. Leaving again locks the profile.
  • Refreshing or leaving the page submits the quiz. Unanswered questions count as wrong.
  • Questions cannot be skipped, and each has a 45-second timer.

When a profile is locked

The quiz is submitted, an alarm sounds, and the student cannot continue until Mr. Gilbert gives a one-time unlock key. Every lock is recorded.

Why: quizzes show what each student really knows, so the work must be their own.

Class expectations

What we expect from every student

Keep up with the weekly plan

Finish each week’s topics and pass the topic quizzes on time.

Aim for 70% or more

Retry any quiz below 70%. Every try is new practice, not memorising.

Do your own work

No copying and no AI tools during quizzes and assessments.

Practise on paper too

Exams are pen and paper, so practise writing code and algorithms by hand.

Ask for help in class

Mr. Gilbert is there to help. Questions are always welcome.

Be a good digital citizen

Balance screen time and protect personal information online.

How parents can help

Simple ways to support learning at home

  • Check grades on School Online after each unit assessment.
  • Ask your child to show you their Weekly plan and My results on the Computing website.
  • Look at Google Classroom together for upcoming work and feedback.
  • Encourage practice on paper as well as on screen before exams.
  • Talk about healthy media balance and keeping personal data safe.
